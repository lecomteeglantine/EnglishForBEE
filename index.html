SPEAKING LAB

Give your research a voice

Practise the English of seminars, field reports and scientific discussion.

60-second field update

Your survey found fewer pollinators in Site B. Explain the finding without claiming causation.

Structure: observation → possible explanation → limitation → next step.

Discuss the evidence

A habitat restoration project reports a 15% increase in plant richness after one year. Is it a success?

Language: “One limitation is…” / “We would need to know whether…”

Mini conference

Present a species, an interaction or a method to a non-specialist audience in two minutes.

Checklist: define key terms, give an example, explain why it matters, finish clearly.

SESSION 1

The Invisible Biodiversity — Environmental DNA

A collaborative mission exploring how researchers collect evidence, interpret eDNA detections and assess the limits of biodiversity monitoring.

Two Methods, One Question

Your challenge: What can scientists conclude about bull trout presence from eDNA and electrofishing?

Work in a group of 2, 3 or 4. Read each evidence card, discuss the options and agree on one decision. Everyone gives a reason before you choose. Read the feedback, then continue.

Teaching case: The methods are authentic, but all sample results below are simulated; they are not Parks Canada's field data.

Field dossier

One river survey investigated three sites. At each site, the team collected three independent water samples for bull trout eDNA and conducted an electrofishing survey. All contamination-control samples were negative.

SiteeDNA samplesElectrofishing
A · Upper reach2 of 3 positiveNo bull trout caught
B · Lower reach3 of 3 positiveOne bull trout caught

A positive eDNA sample means bull trout DNA was detected, not that a fish was captured. No population-size estimate is available.
